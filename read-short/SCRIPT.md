# The Janitor Who Had $8 Million — Ronald Read

Vertical animated short (1080x1920, 30 fps, ~41 s) for the Vacko finance channel. Same cartoon-3d-short style as the
Newton short; English voice-over; word-by-word subtitles burned in; hook text on screen in the first second.

## Facts (with sources)
1. Ronald Read (b. 23 Oct 1921, d. 2 Jun 2014, Brattleboro, Vermont) worked almost 25 years as an attendant and mechanic
   at a service station, then 17 years part-time as a custodian at a J. C. Penney store (until 1997).
2. He drove a used 2007 Toyota Yaris, held his denim jacket together with a safety pin, wore worn flannel shirts and
   gathered his own firewood.
3. A patron at the restaurant where he ate breakfast once paid for his meal, assuming he couldn't afford it.
4. He held at least 95 stocks, mostly dividend-paying blue chips (healthcare, utilities, banking, consumer goods, e.g.
   Johnson & Johnson, Procter & Gamble), reinvested the dividends and held for decades (PG&E shares bought in 1959).
   His stock certificates, kept in a safe deposit box, stacked five inches high.
5. His estate was nearly $8 million. He left $4.8 million to Brattleboro Memorial Hospital and $1.2 million to Brooks
   Memorial Library, the largest gifts either had ever received; friends and neighbours did not know he was wealthy.
Sources: Wikipedia, "Ronald Read (philanthropist)"; Today/NBC, Japan Times, Washington Post (Barry Ritholtz), 2015.
No brand names appear on screen.

## Srpski prevod

3. Vozio je polovan auto i kaput držao zakačen zihernadlom.
