# The $900 Million Typo — idea, script, sources and posting (v1, for approval)

A true-story short for **Vacko | Money Stories**, to be made with the `cartoon-3d-short` skill: in August 2020
Citibank meant to pay Revlon's lenders a $7.8 million interest payment and sent about $900 million instead; a judge
first let lenders keep about $500 million, an appeals court reversed it two years later.

## Facts (sources)
- 11 Aug 2020: Citibank, as Revlon's loan agent, meant to send about $7.8 million of interest and wired about
  $900 million (the full principal) to the lenders; three people approved the payment in the software:
  [Al Jazeera, 16 Feb 2021](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2021/2/16/900m-gaffe-citigroup-cant-recoup-payout-to-revlon-judge-rules),
  [Bloomberg, 8 Sep 2022](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-09-08/court-says-lenders-not-entitled-to-repayment-of-loan).
- Some lenders returned about $385 million; others kept about $500 million ($504 million) and Citi sued:
  [Irish Times / Bloomberg](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2022/09/09/citigroup-wins-us-appeal-over-500m-it-sent-by-mistake/),
  [Banking Dive](https://www.bankingdive.com/news/citi-wins-appeal-500-million-revlon-loan-blunder/631536/).
- Feb 2021: Judge Jesse Furman ruled the lenders could keep it: they "believed, and were justified in believing,
  that the payments were intentional" (the amounts matched what Revlon owed, to the cent) (Al Jazeera).
- 8 Sep 2022: the 2nd Circuit reversed 3-0: the lenders were on notice that it was a mistake
  ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/articles/citigroup-wins-appeal-in-500-million-revlon-transfer-case),
  [Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2022/09/12/684628.htm)).

## Script
1. **Hook** — A bank meant to send seven point eight million dollars. It sent nine hundred million instead.
   — *a clerk presses Enter; a money counter on the screen spins past 7,800,000 and keeps going*
2. **Setting** — August 2020. Citibank was paying the interest on a loan for the makeup company Revlon.
   — *a New York bank tower; lipsticks and a loan contract on a desk*
3. The payment ran through old software, with boxes that had to be ticked exactly right.
   — *a grey screen full of tiny checkboxes*
4. **Action** — Three people checked it. All three approved it.
   — *three employees, one after another, each nods and stamps APPROVED*
5. By the next morning, about nine hundred million dollars had landed with the lenders. The whole loan. Early.
   — *money pours out of the screen into eight lenders' offices*
6. Citi asked for it back. Some lenders returned it. Others kept about half a billion.
   — *some lenders carry bags back; others hug their bags and shake their heads*
7. **Snap** — So the bank went to court. And a federal judge said the lenders could keep the money.
   — *a gavel; the lenders cheer; the Citi man's jaw drops*
8. **Explanation** — Why? Because Revlon really did owe them that money, and it arrived exactly to the cent.
   — *the judge holds up the payment slip beside the loan: the numbers match*
9. It's like a friend who owes you fifty dollars sending exactly fifty dollars by accident. You'd think he finally paid you back.
   — *two friends; a phone buzzes "$50 received"; a happy fist pump*
10. **Deadpan double** — Luckily for Citi, a higher court reversed it two years later. Unluckily, it took two years.
    — *a calendar flips through 24 months; the Citi man ages, grey hair*
11. **Callback twist** — Nine hundred million dollars, sent by a few tiny boxes that three people checked.
    — *back to the checkbox screen; one box glows; the cursor blinks*
