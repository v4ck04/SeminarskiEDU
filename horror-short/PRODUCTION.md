# VOICEMAIL FROM TOMORROW — Complete Production Package

Original horror short for YouTube Shorts · 9:16 · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · 58 seconds
Visual identity: **2D illustrated animated characters composited into a fully 3D cinematic environment.**

---

## PART 1 – ORIGINAL STORY

**Logline:** At 3:12 a.m., a woman gets a voicemail from her own phone number, recorded tomorrow, warning her not to open the door at 3:17. She obeys, and learns too late that the warning was meant to let someone in.

**Story.** Mara Quinn, 27, a night-shift sound editor, comes home after midnight to her small fourth-floor apartment. It is raining. At 3:12 her phone lights up on the kitchen table. It shows *1 new voicemail* from **her own number**.

The message is her own voice, whispering: *"It's me. You're standing in the kitchen. You haven't turned on the light."* She hasn't. She plays it again, and this time she notices the timestamp: *Tomorrow, 3:20 a.m.*

The voice continues: *"At 3:17, someone will knock. It will sound like me. Do not open the door."*

Mara laughs nervously. The oven clock flips from 3:16 to **3:17**. Three knocks. Her own voice comes from the hallway: *"Mara? It's me. Let me in. Please… it's cold."*

She looks through the peephole. The fisheye view shows an empty, flickering hallway. But under the door, in the strip of hallway light, there are the shadows of **two feet**.

She doesn't open. The knocking stops. Her phone buzzes again: one new voicemail. This time the voice is crying: *"Why didn't you let me in? I was trying to warn you… It already got inside. It's standing behind you."*

Mara turns slowly. The kitchen is empty. In the black rain-streaked window, her reflection stands where she stands, wearing her clothes and her face. Mara is not smiling. **The reflection is.** The phone screen dies. Black.

**Twist logic (kept consistent for the comments section):** the first voicemail was not sent by Mara. It was sent by the thing, to make sure she would keep the real Mara (from tomorrow, trying to come back and warn her) outside. The second voicemail is the real Mara, too late.

**Themes:** trusting your own voice; the horror of a closed door doing the wrong job; the doppelgänger.

### 3.2 THE OTHER (the reflection / the thing)

Identical in **every** design detail to Mara (same sheet, same colors, same outline), so viewers can't tell them apart, except:
- Pupils are pinpoints (40 % size) with **no catch-light**.
- Its smile is asymmetric: the left corner rises 8 px higher than the right, with no eye involvement.
- It never blinks.
- It appears only as a reflection (window glass, black phone screen) or as a shadow under the door. It is **never** shown directly in the room.

### 3.3 MARA-FROM-TOMORROW (voice only)

Never shown. Present only as the voice behind the door and on the second voicemail.

**Consistency lock:** one character sheet, used as the only reference for every shot. No changes to hair, cardigan, colors, line weight or proportions between shots.
