[hook] He bet 500 pounds the earth was flat. The man who took that bet regretted it for years.
[idea] England, 1870. John Hampden was sure the world was a flat disc, ringed by a wall of ice.
[offer] So he made an offer, 500 pounds to anyone who could prove water curves.
[laugh] Everyone laughed it off. One man didn't. Alfred Russel Wallace, one of the greatest scientists alive.
[canal] The test, a dead straight canal, six miles long,
[markers] three markers, all at the exact same height. If the water is flat, they line up. If the earth is round, the middle one sticks up.
[scope] Wallace looked through the telescope. The middle marker, sticking up.
[win] The referee called it. Wallace wins.
[fraud] Hampden didn't pay. He called it fraud.
[letters] Then he started writing letters to Wallace, to his friends, to his wife. Your husband is a thief
[years] for years. Threats, lawsuits. Wallace never saw his money.
[prison] And Hampden, he went to prison. Not for being wrong about the earth, for threatening the man who proved it.
