# The $4.5 Trillion Scandal (Jensen Huang and Nvidia)

Vertical animated short (1080x1920, 30 fps, about 55 s) in the same cartoon style as the Newton and Ronald Read shorts.
English voice-over; word-by-word subtitles burned in; hook card "THE $4.5 TRILLION SCANDAL" in the first seconds.
News story, checked on 10 November 2026. Re-check the status of the board review before posting.

## Facts (with sources)
1. On Sunday 8 November 2026 Jensen Huang emailed Nvidia staff: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and
   our children." He wrote that he had "fallen short" of his responsibilities and that he is "deeply sorry".
   (CNBC, 9 Nov 2026; CNN, 8 Nov 2026)
2. The New York Times, Financial Times and Wall Street Journal report that U.S. intelligence agencies believe the woman
   passed information to Beijing. China's Foreign Ministry called the spy claims "groundless" / "unsubstantiated".
   The woman is not named or shown in the video: the claim is unproven. (CNBC; ABC News Australia, 10 Nov 2026)
3. Nvidia's board opened an independent review led by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel.
   The board said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised".
   Nvidia said Huang is "committed to continuing to lead NVIDIA as CEO without interruption". (NBC News; CNBC)
4. On Monday 9 November Nvidia closed down 4.27% (about $178.67). Bloomberg put the loss at nearly $200 billion of
   market value. Before the news Nvidia was worth about $4.5 trillion. (CNBC; Bloomberg; Yahoo Finance)
5. Nvidia is about 7% of the S&P 500 (Yahoo Finance graphic, 9 Nov 2026), so an S&P 500 index fund holds roughly
   7 cents of every dollar in Nvidia.
6. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 with Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem after meeting at a Denny's in San Jose;
   the company started with $40,000. Its first products were graphics chips for PC games; in 1999 it introduced what it
   called the first GPU; its chips went on to power the AI boom. (ABC News Australia; Nvidia company history)
7. Left out on purpose: the shareholder lawsuit (only thinly reported so far), the woman's name and company, and any
   claim about what, if anything, was passed on.
