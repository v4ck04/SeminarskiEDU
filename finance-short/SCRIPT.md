# What Goes Up — Isaac Newton and the South Sea Bubble

Vertical animated short (1080x1920, 30 fps, ~60 s) for a YouTube finance channel.
Style: cartoon-3d-short skill (flat 2D characters in a painted 3D world, three.js in Remotion). English voice-over, no on-screen text.

## Facts (with sources)
1. In 1720 Newton was 77 (born 25 Dec 1642) and Master of the Royal Mint (warden 1696, master 1699 until his death in 1727).
   — A. Odlyzko, "Isaac Newton and the perils of the financial South Sea", *Physics Today*, July 2020.
2. The Royal Mint worked inside the Tower of London until 1810. — Royal Mint history; Tower of London history.
3. South Sea Company shares: about £128 in January 1720, £330 in March, £550 at the end of May, £890 in early June,
   £1,000 in early August, £150 at the end of September, about £100 by December.
   — Wikipedia, "South Sea Company" (citing Carswell, *The South Sea Bubble*; Dale, *The First Crash*).
4. Newton sold his South Sea shares in April 1720 (19 and 23 April) and made an early profit of about £20,000.
   — Odlyzko 2020.
5. In June 1720 he sold government bonds and bought South Sea stock again, "almost at the peak", and kept buying as the
   price slid; by the September collapse nearly his entire fortune was in South Sea stock. — Odlyzko 2020.
6. His net worth was just over £30,000 before the bubble and about £20,000 by mid-1721: he lost all his early profit and
   more. — Odlyzko 2020.
7. Newton wrote to Fatio de Duillier: "I lost very much by the South Sea company which makes my pockets empty".
   — quoted in Odlyzko 2020.
8. Chancellor of the Exchequer John Aislabie was expelled from the Commons and sent to the Tower of London on
   9 March 1721. — Wikipedia, "John Aislabie"; "1721 in Great Britain".
9. Left out on purpose: the line "I can calculate the motions of the heavenly bodies, but not the madness of people".
   It is only *attributed* to Newton and has no contemporary source.

## Script (voice-over, one steady deadpan tone)
| # | Beat | Line |
|---|------|------|
| 1 | Hook | Isaac Newton explained why everything that goes up must come down. Then he lost a fortune betting it wouldn't. |
| 2 | Setting | London, seventeen twenty. Newton is seventy-seven, and runs the Royal Mint. He literally makes England's money. |
| 3 | Action 1 | Everyone is buying shares of the South Sea Company. By spring, the price has nearly tripled. |
| 4 | Action 2 | Newton sells in April, and walks away twenty thousand pounds richer. |
| 5 | Action 3 | But the price keeps climbing. And his friends keep getting richer. |
| 6 | Action 4 | In June, the smartest man in Europe can't take it anymore. He buys back in, with almost everything he owns. |
| 7 | Snap | In August, one share hits a thousand pounds. In September, it falls off a cliff. |
| 8 | Deadpan | Luckily, Newton had sold early. Unluckily, he also bought back late. |
| 9 | Explanation | A bubble is like a ball thrown in the air. It only rises while someone keeps pushing. Here, the push was new buyers. When they ran out, gravity took over. |
| 10 | Consequence | Parliament locked the finance minister in the Tower of London, the same fortress where Newton made the coins. |
| 11 | Consequence 2 | Newton lost all his profit, and then some. He wrote that the South Sea left his pockets empty. |
| 12 | Callback | So if the man who explained gravity couldn't time the market, maybe you don't need to either. |

## Srpski prevod (za titl, opis ili snimanje sopstvenog glasa)
1. Isak Njutn je objasnio zašto sve što ode gore mora da padne. A onda je izgubio bogatstvo kladeći se da neće.
2. London, 1720. Njutn ima sedamdeset sedam godina i vodi Kraljevsku kovnicu. Bukvalno pravi novac Engleske.
3. Ceo grad kupuje akcije kompanije South Sea. Do proleća, cena se skoro utrostručila.
4. Njutn u aprilu prodaje sve i odlazi bogatiji za dvadeset hiljada funti.
5. Ali cena nastavlja da raste. I njegovi prijatelji su sve bogatiji. Svake nedelje.
6. U junu, najpametniji čovek Evrope to više ne može da gleda. Ponovo kupuje, i kupuje, dok skoro sve što ima nije u tim akcijama.
7. U avgustu, jedna akcija dostiže hiljadu funti. U septembru, pada u provaliju.
8. Srećom, Njutn je prodao rano. Nažalost, i kupio je ponovo kasno.
9. Balon je kao lopta bačena uvis. Raste samo dok je neko gura nagore. Ovde su guranje bili novi kupci. Kad su ponestali, gravitacija je preuzela.
10. Parlament je zatvorio ministra finansija u Londonsku kulu. Istu tvrđavu u kojoj je Njutn kovao novac.
11. Njutn je izgubio sav profit, i više od toga. Napisao je da su mu akcije South Sea ispraznile džepove.
12. Pa ako čovek koji je objasnio gravitaciju nije umeo da pogodi pravi trenutak na berzi, možda ne moraš ni ti.
